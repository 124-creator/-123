# Cover letter — draft for human-author approval, NOT sent

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit “Bid and award dispersion in European carbon auctions” for consideration as a research article in Empirical Economics.

The manuscript examines what public primary-auction quantity statistics reveal jointly. Using 1,281 successful EUA auctions from 2020–2025, it estimates the association between reported bid and award dispersion after accounting for scale, participation, auction series and calendar-month effects. The residual correlation is approximately 0.65 under two transformations. The same direction remains in the completed control and influence checks, but the association is stronger in 2023–2025 than in 2020–2022. This temporal qualification is central to the paper rather than a secondary robustness claim.

The study distinguishes the arithmetic of ratio denominators from the empirical variation used to estimate the relationship. It is positioned as an empirical measurement study, complementing price-focused auction work. It does not claim causal allocation compression, bidder-level inequality, market power or forecasting gains. The historical, exploratory design and the unresolved population metadata are stated explicitly.

The manuscript, supplementary analyses and numerical provenance provide a reproducible account of the selected historical archive. We believe the empirical relationships and measurement focus are relevant to the journal's readership. The final data-access statement will respect the original provider's conditions.
