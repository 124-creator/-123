# Cover letter — author-review draft, not sent

Dear Editors,

We would like to submit “Bid and award dispersion in European carbon auctions” for consideration as a research article in Empirical Economics.

The paper asks how two public primary-auction quantity indicators relate after accounting for auction scale and participation, and whether one pooled relationship adequately describes the historical archive. Using 1,281 successful EUA auctions from 2020–2025, it documents a residual correlation of approximately 0.65 under two transformations. The positive direction remains under the completed control and influence checks, while the association is stronger in 2023–2025 than in 2020–2022.

The contribution is a conditional and period-specific description of reported quantity information. We distinguish denominator identities from estimated association and do not treat correlation as identification of a common latent construct or evidence that the indicators are interchangeable. The study complements price-focused auction research. Its results are not presented as causal allocation compression, bidder-level inequality or forecasting gains.

The manuscript provides explicit measures, completed sensitivity analyses and reproducible numerical provenance. It discloses the historical, exploratory design, the limitations of month-block inference and the unresolved populations underlying reported standard deviations. We believe this empirical measurement question is relevant to the journal’s readership. Source data remain subject to the provider’s rights and the final access arrangement will be stated accurately.
