Dear Editors of Empirical Economics,

Please consider our manuscript, “Quantity dispersion and reported price deviations in European carbon auctions: Conditional associations and historical scope,” by Zhongfei Tian and Xiang Wang, as a research article.

The study asks two related empirical questions: how published bid and award dispersion co-vary after accounting for auction scale and participation, and whether bid dispersion has a price-related association beyond conventional auction characteristics. The quantity sample contains 1,281 successful EUA auctions during 2020–2025. A separate matched sample of 240 German auctions uses the price comparisons printed in official reports.

The quantity relationship is positive but historically heterogeneous. In the German sample, an observed interquartile range of transformed bid dispersion corresponds to a fitted signed-deviation difference of −14.51 basis points, with a quarter-clustered 95% interval of [−23.00,−6.01]. We report the absolute-outcome and source-subset analyses alongside this finding: the absolute-deviation interval includes zero, and omitting 2022 materially attenuates the signed coefficient. The paper therefore concerns conditional relationships in reported statistics, not causal pricing efficiency or trading returns.

The manuscript provides explicit measurement definitions, common-sample benchmarks and uncertainty diagnostics. The analysis code, aggregate outputs and source catalogue are publicly documented at fixed repository revisions. Matched event-level inputs remain subject to third-party rights; the data statement explains both the public and conditional-access routes. Generative AI assistance is disclosed in the methods, and the authors retain responsibility for the paper.

We believe the paper fits the journal's interest in empirical economic relationships and measurement. The research received no financial support. Our preferred publication route is traditional subscription rather than optional open access.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
